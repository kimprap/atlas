# Atlas ask orchestration

**Datetime**: 2026-08-03-0002
**Scope**: Atlas public entrypoint, Atlas Orchestrator/Workflow Owner coordination, defect-to-engineering escalation, bounded Engineering Cycles, learning candidates, documentation, and assurance
**Summary**: Replace the current ten-command Atlas surface with one `/atlas-ask` interface. The capable main-session Atlas Orchestrator is the sole top-level Atlas/engineering coordinator: it delegates one complete Atlas goal to one existing Workflow Owner subagent, consumes one identity-bound strict result, guides only Workflow Owner-authorized narrow stops, and routes reproducible framework defects through separately approved same-session Engineering Cycles. The initial Atlas approval explicitly authorizes at most three receipt-gated fresh executions of the unchanged goal and write set; existing façade, reducer, transaction, recovery, Workflow Owner, and living-vault behavior remain unchanged.
**Status**: BLOCKED

## Context

Today `/atlas` is a stateless first-owner router, every expert `atlas-*` skill also has a direct public command, and only `atlas-source` defines a strict Semantic Worker wave. The router cannot inspect Atlas state, run a workflow, retry, recover, or chain owners; no generic Workflow Owner transport, Atlas Orchestrator result contract, Atlas-to-engineering evidence seam, or active memory backend exists.

The approved product decisions for this plan are:

- **Orchestration model**: the main-session Atlas Orchestrator dispatches one expert Workflow Owner subagent. That Workflow Owner executes its deep existing procedure and only the Semantic Worker waves or Successor Owner chains already authorized by its owner module. The Atlas Orchestrator is the sole top-level run and engineering coordinator; it never performs expert semantic work or Atlas façade/vault/reducer/transaction work.
- **Public surface**: strict cutover to `/atlas-ask`. Remove `/atlas` and all nine direct expert command stubs. Move the nine expert workflows beneath the `atlas-ask` skill as non-discovered internal Workflow Owner modules with stable `atlas-*` owner IDs; keep `atlas-core` hidden and support-only. No aliases, top-level expert-skill commands, or compatibility bypasses.
- **Defect route and retry authority**: when a Workflow Owner returns reproducible product-framework defect evidence, the Atlas Orchestrator first presents a short batched issue/effect summary, then renders the existing exact `dev-ask` Route Overview and waits for its separate approval. No engineering stage runs under Atlas approval. The initial actionable Atlas overview includes an exact `Retry` line authorizing at most three receipt-gated fresh Atlas executions after separately approved Engineering Cycles, only for the unchanged goal and write set.
- **Loop bound**: one original Atlas run may enter at most three completed Engineering Cycles, yielding at most four Atlas executions. Each Engineering Cycle is a separately approved `dev-ask` lifecycle and may end `repaired` or `no-repair`. A valid receipt triggers a visible status message and immediate fresh execution under the initial Atlas approval; it does not request redundant Atlas approval. Existing `dev-implementation` attempt limits remain separate and unchanged. A repeated identical defect without a new falsifiable hypothesis, unsafe/ambiguous effects, stale authority, or an exhausted engineering route stops earlier.
- **Workflow Owner resume bound**: shared transport uses the largest owner-native ceiling, not one arbitrary cap. `atlas-source`, `atlas-acquire`, and `atlas-research` may consume at most two batched material-answer resumes plus one contract-guidance resume (`owner_turn <= 4`); every other Workflow Owner permits no post-dispatch material Ask and at most one contract-guidance resume (`owner_turn <= 2`). Stable bounded-step IDs plus an ephemeral all-prior-turn progress-key check stop exact repeats and short cycles. The source Semantic Worker’s existing one correction is owner-internal and does not consume `owner_turn`.
- **Learning**: recurring faulty behavior and repeated custom lookup/tool sequences may appear only as one redacted, non-authoritative learning candidate in the ephemeral result. This change does not activate or write the provisional repository memory system.
- **Decision rationale**: accepted architecture rationale lives under `docs/adr/`, with a compact conditional `INDEX.md`. ADRs are never injected or scanned by default; `.agents/ARCHITECTURE.md` remains normative.

No task in this plan changes the deterministic Atlas façade operation set, canonical Markdown state, connector/profile behavior, reducer semantics, mode write sets, transaction/recovery protocols, source-worker packet, or living research data.

### Canonical orchestration vocabulary

- **Atlas Orchestrator**: the main-session `/atlas-ask` controller. It classifies, obtains approval, dispatches and validates one Workflow Owner result, presents outcomes, and coordinates a separately approved engineering route; it performs no expert Atlas work.
- **Workflow Owner**: the one selected expert module and subagent that owns an end-to-end Atlas workflow.
- **Successor Owner**: a Workflow Owner invoked through an allowlisted owner-internal chain while the selected Workflow Owner retains the outer result.
- **Semantic Worker**: an owner-local worker in an already-authorized worker wave; it is not a Workflow Owner or a top-level coordinator.
- **Engineering Cycle**: one separately approved `dev-ask` lifecycle entered for a proven framework defect. It may end with a product repair or with a terminal no-repair finding.
- **Engineering Cycle Receipt**: ephemeral terminal engineering evidence that permits one fresh Atlas execution under the initial bounded retry authority and consumes one of the three Engineering Cycles.

## Approach

`atlas-ask` is one deep public module over the existing Workflow Owners:

```text
user request
  → atlas-ask classification / material Ask / Goal-Next-Effect-Retry approval
  → one identity-bound strict Workflow Owner Task
      → Workflow Owner-native façade work and authorized Semantic Workers/Successor Owners
  ← one strict aggregate workflow result
  → present result | Ask/resume same Workflow Owner | stop | approved dev-ask Engineering Cycle
```

Every actionable overview renders exactly:

```text
Goal: <one-sentence interpretation of the unchanged request>
Next: <one selected Workflow Owner and its first bounded action>
Effect: <read-only; no Atlas writes | write-capable through the selected Workflow Owner; exact effects remain owner-gated>
Retry: up to 3 receipt-gated fresh Atlas executions after separately approved Engineering Cycles; unchanged goal and write set only.

Approve this Atlas route?
```

Classification-only and no-owner outcomes do not render an approval block.

For approval and retry binding, a write-capable `Effect` means the exact maximum logical owner/mode write-set allowed by the canonical architecture and selected Workflow Owner, derived only from the unchanged request and any pre-route material answers. It names path/registry classes and authorized Successor Owner modes, not state-dependent target identities, bytes, or revisions. Retry equality compares this logical boundary; realized targets remain owner-gated. If the Atlas Orchestrator cannot derive the owner, mode, and maximum logical boundary without Atlas I/O or semantic workflow work, it must Ask for the missing material choice or stop before the actionable overview.

The Atlas Orchestrator sends one exact Task input composed of the compact dispatch binding below, the unchanged original request, the retained pre-route material answers (possibly empty), later Workflow Owner-requested material answers, bounded orchestrator-guidance answers, compact prior-defect references, and the strict result schema. It retains that semantic input, the approved logical owner/mode write-set boundary, and the Task tool’s returned delivery handle out of band and accepts a result only from that handle; the Workflow Owner does not self-attest its transport identity. The Atlas Orchestrator-generated opaque `dispatch_id` identifies only this approved Workflow Owner Task and is not a mutation token, persisted baton, or reducer authority. The input contains no Atlas state projection, target bytes, reducer event, transaction identity, resolved profile/root, synthetic mutation authority, or Atlas Orchestrator-authored semantic output. The Atlas Orchestrator retains approved Engineering Cycle Receipts only through exact same-session Handoff references to enforce the outer ceiling; they are not sent as Atlas authority.

The Workflow Owner result echoes only the compact dispatch binding: `dispatch_id`, owner, Atlas-attempt ordinal, owner-turn ordinal, and exact architecture/guardrail/result-schema/owner-module revisions. The Atlas Orchestrator requires delivery from the retained Task/agent handle and compares every binding field with its retained Task input and current Context Pack. The initial result uses `owner_turn:1`. A validated same-Workflow-Owner resume keeps every binding field byte-identical except `owner_turn`, which increments by one; the Task input preserves the pre-route and prior resume answers and appends only the exact new `material_answers` for the preceding `needs-user` questions or the exact new `guidance_answers` for the preceding `needs-guidance` question. Results never echo the request, answers, or prior-defect records. A wrong handle, skipped/repeated turn, owner-ceiling breach, stale or repeated resume key, mismatch, stale revision, missing field, foreign result, or invalid answer binding fails closed as an Atlas Orchestrator-owned transport stop—not a reconstructed workflow result—and never reaches user-result or `dev-ask` routing.

Same-handle resume is finite and owner-native:

| Selected Workflow Owner | Allowed post-dispatch `needs-user` resumes | Allowed `needs-guidance` resumes | Maximum `owner_turn` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `atlas-source`, `atlas-acquire`, `atlas-research` | Two: one batched depth-1 aggregate-expansion choice, then one batched source-worker material choice | One | 4 |
| `atlas-route`, `atlas-refresh`, `atlas-answer`, `atlas-audit`, `atlas-index`, `atlas-migrate` | Zero; unresolved material scope is handled before approval or by a new invocation | One | 2 |

The `atlas-ask` intent map owns stable pre-route question/option IDs, and the source owner contract fixes the inherited resume step IDs `source.aggregate-expansion` then `source.worker-choice`. For every `needs-user|needs-guidance` result, the Atlas Orchestrator computes an ephemeral prose-insensitive resume key from canonical JSON of `{outcome, guidance.kind, question IDs, multi flags, option IDs/recommended flags, evidence refs}` and compares it with every prior resume key for that Workflow Owner Task. A repeated key, an out-of-order or unknown source step ID, or an exhausted owner ceiling returns `blocked`; keys are never persisted. Non-source owners permit at most one guidance resume, so arbitrary ID churn cannot extend a Task. The existing source same-Semantic-Worker correction is not a Workflow Owner resume.

The human README will use this table’s plain-language intents and examples; users will not need to know or invoke internal owner names.

### Invocation and Workflow Owner-chain boundary

The nine Workflow Owner procedures move to ordinary resources at `.agents/skills/atlas-ask/owners/<owner-id>/OWNER.md`. Because only `atlas-ask/SKILL.md` retains behavior-skill semantics, neither one-level nor recursive skill discovery registers commands for the nine legacy owner IDs. Each owner resource retains an intake guard and accepts exactly two qualified call forms:

1. the selected Workflow Owner receives a live `atlas-ask` Workflow Owner Task with the matching dispatch binding; or
2. an allowlisted current Workflow Owner loads and executes a required Successor Owner resource in the same agent while carrying the same root binding and its `internal_caller` identity.

Loading an internal Workflow Owner module outside either form must perform no read, model work, façade call, or write and return a short redirect to `/atlas-ask`. This defends manual asset loading without relying on prompt text to hide an otherwise discovered command.

Only the selected Workflow Owner wraps once when returning to the Atlas Orchestrator. Internal acquire→source, source→route, and research→acquire/refresh calls retain their current native input/output contracts inside that same Workflow Owner agent; Successor Owners never create another Workflow Owner Task, emit, or nest the outer workflow result. The selected Workflow Owner consumes those native results, preserves its compact current output, and emits one aggregate outer result.

Only owner-local Semantic Worker waves and isolated calibration tasks use nested Task dispatch, at depth 2. Before dispatching a route that may require them, the Atlas Orchestrator must live-verify task recursion depth of at least 2; unavailable depth returns `capability-unavailable` before Workflow Owner work. Owners whose complete route requires no nested task need only the ordinary depth-1 Workflow Owner capability.

### Workflow result and failure policy

Add `atlas-workflow-result.schema.json` under `atlas-core/assets` as the sole owner of the compact dispatch binding, shared Task-input record shapes, exact result transport, and defect payload. It is a Draft 2020-12, closed, reference-free schema asset, not a façade operation, vault artifact, reducer event, run ledger, or engineering Handoff. The root result uses a closed discriminated `oneOf` so `outcome` alone determines the legal payload and Atlas Orchestrator action; `$defs.dispatch-input` owns the parent-to-Workflow-Owner input shape without becoming part of the echoed result.

| Outcome | Required meaning and payload | Atlas Orchestrator action |
|---|---|---|
| `completed` | All required Workflow Owner work is terminal; aggregate effect is `none` or `committed`. It may summarize committed and terminal no-write/rejected siblings. | Present compact Workflow Owner summary; no generic assurance. |
| `no-write` | All required work is terminal no-write/rejected and aggregate effect is exactly `none`. | Present the no-write reason. |
| `needs-user` | No defect or unsafe effect; one or more Workflow Owner-defined material choices are batched. Payload contains exact bounded questions/options/recommendations and one safe resume condition. | Use one host Ask, then resume the same Workflow Owner when transport remains available. |
| `needs-guidance` | No user/product choice or defect; one precise contract/evidence question can be answered mechanically from already-bound product contracts or conversation evidence, without semantic work or new authority. | Answer with exact evidence references and resume the same Workflow Owner; if the answer needs Atlas-state inspection, semantic inference, new authority, or unavailable evidence, ask the user or stop. |
| `blocked` | Capability/transport/external/Workflow Owner blocker or aggregate effect `partial|unknown`; payload contains the exact blocker and Workflow Owner-owned resume/recovery condition. | Stop or invoke only the Workflow Owner’s already-authorized recovery. The sole exception is the exact recovery-defect tuple below: it may route a separately approved Engineering Cycle against product recovery code, but it permits no bound-instance engineering write and no receipt-gated Atlas retry unless fresh native recovery is proven reconstructible. |
| `framework-defect` | Aggregate effect is safely `none|committed`; one or more reproducible product discrepancies remain after Workflow Owner correction/recovery. Defects carry exact contract/target revisions, expected/observed behavior, stable signatures, already-run reproductions, current/changed falsifiable hypotheses, and affected/continuing branches; the aggregate carries the sole safe resume. | Batch and route to a separately approved `dev-ask` Engineering Cycle. |

#### Exact wire schema

Every object is closed with `additionalProperties:false`; every listed field is required; every unqualified string has `minLength:1`. `revision` means a string matching `^sha256:[0-9a-f]{64}$`. `owner` is exactly one of the nine IDs in the intent map. Arrays described as sets use `uniqueItems:true`.

| Field | Exact type and cardinality |
|---|---|
| `version` | integer constant `1` |
| `dispatch` | exact `dispatch-binding` object below |
| `outcome` | `completed|no-write|needs-user|needs-guidance|blocked|framework-defect`; each `oneOf` branch constrains it to one constant |
| `summary` | string |
| `items` | array, `minItems:1`, of exact `{id:string,status:item-status,summary:string}`; item-status is `completed|no-write|rejected|needs-user|needs-guidance|blocked|framework-defect` |
| `aggregate_effect` | `none|committed|partial|unknown` |
| `resume` | `null|string` as constrained per outcome below |
| `guidance` | `null|user-guidance|orchestrator-guidance` as constrained below |
| `blocker` | `null|blocker` as constrained below |
| `defects` | array of exact defect objects; cardinality is outcome-specific |
| `learning_candidate` | `null|learning-candidate`; legal on every outcome and never changes routing |

Nested definitions are exact:

- `ref = {uri:string,revision:revision}`.
- `signature = {owner:owner,contract_uri:string,target_uris:string-set,finding_code:string,failure_boundary:string}` where `target_uris.minItems:1`, producers emit `target_uris` in lexicographic order, and `finding_code` matches `^[A-Z][A-Z0-9_]{1,63}$`. Exact object equality—not a model-generated hash—is recurrence identity. Stable target URIs distinguish different artifacts while contract/target revision changes remain comparable.
- `hypothesis = {id:string,statement:string,changed_from:null|string}`. `changed_from` names the prior hypothesis ID; a new ID without a changed falsifiable statement is not a changed hypothesis.
- `material-answer = {question_id:string,selected_ids:string-set,free_text:null|string}`. `selected_ids` may be empty and `free_text` null individually, but an `anyOf` requires at least one selected ID or non-null free text.
- `guidance-answer = {question_id:string,answer:string,evidence_refs:ref-set}` with `evidence_refs.minItems:1`.
- `prior-defect = {id:string,signature:signature,targets:ref-set,hypothesis:hypothesis}` with `targets.minItems:1` and sorted target URIs equal to `signature.target_uris`. This bounded record is carried into every fresh Workflow Owner Task.
- `dispatch-binding = {dispatch_id:string,owner:owner,atlas_attempt:integer 1..4,owner_turn:integer 1..4,architecture_revision:revision,guardrail_revision:revision,result_schema_revision:revision,owner_revision:revision}`. The Atlas Orchestrator additionally enforces the owner-specific maximum in the resume table.
- `dispatch-input = {dispatch:dispatch-binding,request:string,material_answers:material-answer-set,guidance_answers:guidance-answer-set,prior_defect_refs:prior-defect-set}`. The initial input uses the exact retained pre-route `material_answers` (possibly empty), empty `guidance_answers`, and `owner_turn:1`. Same-handle resume preserves every prior answer, appends exactly one newly authorized answer per preceding question ID, and increments `owner_turn` by one.
- `option = {id:string,label:string,description:string,recommended:boolean}`. Each question has `2..5` options and uses `contains` with `minContains:1,maxContains:1` on `recommended:true`; zero or multiple recommendations are invalid.
- `user-guidance = {kind:"user",questions:[{id:string,header:null|string,question:string,multi:boolean,options:option[2..5]}]}` with `questions.minItems:1`; the Workflow Owner batches every material question from the bounded invocation.
- `orchestrator-guidance = {kind:"orchestrator",id:string,question:string,evidence_refs:ref-set}` with `evidence_refs.minItems:1`.
- `blocker = {kind:capability|transport|external|owner|recovery,detail:string}`.
- `reproduction = {steps:[string,...],result:string}` with `steps.minItems:1`; step order is significant.
- `defect = {id:string,signature:signature,contract_revision:revision,targets:ref-set,expected:string,observed:string,reproduction:reproduction,hypothesis:hypothesis,affected_branches:string-set,continuing_branches:string-set}` with `targets.minItems:1` and `affected_branches.minItems:1`; `continuing_branches` may be empty. The lexicographically sorted unique URIs from `targets` must equal `signature.target_uris`. The sole outer `aggregate_effect` and `resume` are authoritative.
- `learning-candidate = {kind:faulty-behavior|lookup-pattern,summary:string,evidence_refs:ref-set,frequency:integer >= 2,redactions:string-set}` with `evidence_refs.minItems:1`; `redactions` may be empty. It contains no raw secret, raw evidence body, packet, or state projection.

The six `oneOf` branches use this total tuple matrix; “contains X” means JSON Schema `contains` with `minContains:1`:

| Outcome | Allowed item statuses and required member | `aggregate_effect` | `resume` | `guidance` | `blocker` | `defects` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `completed` | `completed|no-write|rejected`; contains `completed` | `none|committed` | `null` | `null` | `null` | empty |
| `no-write` | `no-write|rejected` | `none` | `null` | `null` | `null` | empty |
| `needs-user` | `completed|no-write|rejected|needs-user|needs-guidance|blocked`; contains `needs-user` | `none|committed` | string | user-guidance | `null` | empty |
| `needs-guidance` | `completed|no-write|rejected|needs-guidance|blocked`; contains `needs-guidance` | `none|committed` | string | orchestrator-guidance | `null` | empty |
| `blocked` | ordinarily `completed|no-write|rejected|blocked`; contains `blocked`. The recovery-defect subcase also permits and contains `framework-defect`. | any allowed value | string | `null` | blocker | empty, except recovery-defect subcase requires `minItems:1` |
| `framework-defect` | any item status; contains `framework-defect` | `none|committed` | string | `null` | `null` | `minItems:1` |


Every variant also carries the exact dispatch binding, Workflow Owner-native compact summary, one authoritative aggregate effect/resume object, and a nullable learning candidate. `needs-user`/`needs-guidance` alone carry guidance. `framework-defect` always carries a nonempty defect array. A `blocked` result may carry defects only in the exact recovery-defect subcase: `aggregate_effect` is `partial|unknown`, `blocker.kind` is `recovery`, `items` contains both `blocked` and `framework-defect`, and `defects.minItems:1`; every other `blocked` result has no `framework-defect` item and an empty defect array. Other variants prohibit defects. There is no independent `next` field and no duplicated effect/resume field inside defect rows.

Schema validation, retained-handle identity, exact dispatch-binding/current-revision comparison, unique item/question/defect IDs, pre-route and resume answer-to-question/selected-option binding, monotonic answer-set comparison, owner-specific turn/transition enforcement, all-prior-turn resume-key comparison, and deterministic equality between each defect’s sorted target URIs and `signature.target_uris` are the only Atlas Orchestrator acceptance checks. These checks bind transport and cross-record invariants JSON Schema cannot express; they are not a second parser or semantic defect validator.

Mixed Workflow Owner results use this exact precedence: `partial|unknown effect → blocked`; otherwise any proven product defect → `framework-defect`; otherwise unresolved user choice → `needs-user`; otherwise eligible narrow Atlas Orchestrator question → `needs-guidance`; otherwise any blocker → `blocked`; otherwise all-terminal/no-commit no-write → `no-write`; otherwise `completed`.

Use the host’s live-verified native strict Task output schema. A contract-equivalent adapter is acceptable only if it validates the same complete object before consumption. Missing structured dispatch/validation is `capability-unavailable`; the Atlas Orchestrator stops before Workflow Owner work and must not fall back to prompt-only parsing, direct Workflow Owner invocation, a removed compatibility command, or doing the expert work itself. Same-Workflow-Owner messaging is recorded at dispatch but becomes a hard capability only if the returned variant requests `needs-user` or `needs-guidance`; if that original Task/agent handle cannot resume, return `blocked` with the smallest fresh-invocation condition. The exact owner transition table and all-prior-turn resume-key check apply before every further message. A new Workflow Owner requires a new `/atlas-ask` overview and approval and is never represented as continuation. One-shot completed/no-write/blocked runs remain valid without same-owner messaging.

Failure classification stays narrow:

1. Semantic Worker evidence/content failure, Workflow Owner validation finding, ordinary no-write, user choice, connector/capability failure, CAS conflict, and known partial-effect recovery remain with the Workflow Owner and its existing limits.
2. The Atlas Orchestrator may answer only a precise `needs-guidance` request mechanically from the already-bound product contract/Context Pack or conversation evidence and must return exact evidence references; it never reads Atlas state for the Workflow Owner. A semantic choice, new authority, changed write set, unavailable evidence, or unavailable same-Workflow-Owner resume goes to the user or stops; it never becomes a generic retry.
3. Independent siblings continue only where the Workflow Owner already declares that safe (for example, valid source siblings after one source fails). A shared transport/contract assumption or ambiguous effect stops its affected cone. The Workflow Owner returns all completed and blocked outcomes together so the Atlas Orchestrator can batch-report them.
4. `framework-defect` requires a reproducible discrepancy against settled product-owned behavior after the Workflow Owner’s normal correction/recovery boundary. One-off model/provider failure, invalid input, missing permission, user authority, or external evidence failure is not a framework defect.
5. The Atlas Orchestrator groups defects by owning contract and stable signature and reports safe completed work and exact effects. It presents one `dev-ask` Route Overview only when one engineering route can safely own every grouped defect. Incompatible authorities or dependency shapes produce one batched report and one human sequencing choice; they never launch multiple engineering routes automatically.
6. A `blocked` recovery-defect result is eligible for a separately approved Engineering Cycle only after the Workflow Owner attempted its authorized recovery and proved a discrepancy in product-owned recovery behavior. Engineering may diagnose, change, smoke, verify, and review only the product recovery contract/code; it may not mutate the bound instance. Automatic Atlas retry is separately gated: terminal engineering evidence must prove that a fresh Workflow Owner can reconstruct the exact native recovery request from the unchanged request/material answers plus existing durable owner state through already-authorized owner/façade reads. Ephemeral target bytes, transaction IDs, projections, or recovery payloads from the old Task are unavailable. If reconstruction is unproven, engineering may finish, but no Engineering Cycle Receipt is issued and the Atlas Orchestrator stops with the exact manual recovery condition.
7. The initial actionable Atlas overview includes the exact `Retry` line and grants up to three receipt-gated fresh executions of the unchanged original goal and logical owner/mode write-set. Every Engineering Cycle still requires its own `dev-ask` Route Overview and human approval. A valid terminal Engineering Cycle Receipt makes the Atlas Orchestrator render `Engineering Cycle <n>/3 <repaired|no-repair>; retrying the approved Atlas goal with current product revisions.`, re-run classification and capability checks, and immediately dispatch a fresh Workflow Owner execution only when owner, mode, and logical write-set remain equal. It never asks for redundant Atlas approval, resumes the pre-defect Workflow Owner Task, or reuses stale Semantic Worker packets or pre-engineering state. The fresh Workflow Owner obtains current product revisions and fresh owner-native inventory/projections. A changed request, route, owner mode, logical write-set, material choice, safety condition, or human/product authority invalidates the bounded retry clause and requires a new Atlas overview and approval.
8. One Engineering Cycle is consumed only when a separately approved `dev-ask` route reaches terminal evidence that permits another Atlas execution and emits a valid receipt, whether code changed or diagnosis established that no product repair was warranted. Approval, dispatch, and internal engineering attempts do not consume an outer cycle. A failed, blocked, inconclusive, non-reconstructible-recovery, or otherwise non-receipted engineering route stops and cannot be repeated under the same approval. A same-signature recurrence may enter another cycle only when the product target revision changed or the terminal evidence supplies a genuinely changed falsifiable hypothesis linked through `changed_from`; the same target plus the same hypothesis stops immediately. The global counter never resets after a repair. Stop after three receipts and at most four Atlas executions. Return one batched terminal report with completed work, defect history, effects/recovery state, and the smallest resume condition.

An Engineering Cycle Receipt is the ephemeral exact object `{cycle:integer 1..3,route_revision:revision,engineering_result_revision:revision,targets:ref-set,outcome:repaired|no-repair,recovery:null|recovery-proof}` derived only from current terminal engineering evidence that permits the next fresh Atlas execution under the initial bounded retry authority. `recovery-proof = {owner:owner,mode:string,evidence_refs:ref-set}` with `evidence_refs.minItems:1`; it is required only for a prior recovery-defect with `partial|unknown` effect and proves exact native recovery-input reconstruction from the unchanged dispatch plus existing durable state. Other receipts require `recovery:null`. `cycle` is globally sequential and never resets; `route_revision` identifies the separately human-approved Engineering Cycle route; `targets.minItems:1` binds every current produced or inspected product target in lexicographic URI order. The receipt neither grants mutation authority nor replaces either human approval.
